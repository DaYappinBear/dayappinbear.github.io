<pre class="story-text">CHESS
How Territorial Can Humans Become?

==================================================
1. THE PREMISE
==================================================

Chess is a serious, bleak tragedy about anthropomorphic chess pieces and their lives. It explores territorialism, hatred, racism, violence, selfishness, manipulation, and the lack of unity among those who are supposed to be on the same side.

The story takes place in Cuboboard, an infinite plane consisting of black and white squares. Two kingdoms, the Albi and the Noir, live facing each other across this endless chessboard.

The Albi are the white kingdom, while the Noir are the black kingdom. Both kingdoms intensely hate each other, despite not having particularly strong reasons for their hatred. Their primary objective is to take over Cuboboard.

Small battles occur constantly between the kingdoms. Initially, these conflicts do not amount to an all-out war, as neither kingdom is willing to sacrifice its entire army without reason. However, their hostility and desire for territorial expansion gradually lead to increasingly destructive confrontations.

The story also explores the lack of unity within each kingdom. The pieces do not necessarily trust, respect, or remain loyal to their own teammates. Their selfishness, disagreements, and conflicting interests contribute to the destruction of both kingdoms.

==================================================
2. THE WORLD: CUBOBOARD
==================================================

Cuboboard is an infinite chessboard with no visible end.

The sky is perpetually dark grey and cloudy, becoming even darker at night. Fog is present throughout the landscape.

THE ALBI KINGDOM:

At one end of Cuboboard stands the Albi kingdom.

Its main castle divides the royal residences. Beside the main castle is the palace of Albus Rex, the white King. Beside his palace is the palace of Queen Regina Alba, the white Queen.

The throne room is located upstairs in the main castle. Both the King and Queen can sit there and watch the events unfolding across Cuboboard.

An army of Pedos, or Pawns, stands in front of the kingdom.

THE NOIR KINGDOM:

The Noir kingdom occupies the opposite end of Cuboboard. It follows a similar arrangement to the Albi kingdom, with its own King, Queen, castle, royal residences, throne room, and Pawn army.

The main difference between the kingdoms is their colour and the personalities of their individual characters.

THE CENTRAL BATTLEFIELD AND GRAVEYARDS:

Between the two kingdoms lies a central battlefield where their soldiers and other chess pieces fight.

On the eastern and western sides of the central battlefield are graveyards for fallen soldiers and chess pieces.

THE RULES OF CUBOBOARD:

The rules of real chess apply to this world. The pieces must obey their respective movement restrictions and other applicable chess rules.

The characters are living beings, but their existence is governed by the mechanics of the game.

==================================================
3. THE KINGDOMS AND THEIR CONFLICT
==================================================

The Albi and Noir want to take over Cuboboard. Their desire for territorial control drives their hostility and repeated battles.

Both kingdoms display intense hatred and racism towards the opposing side. Their members are often ruthless, violent, and lacking in empathy.

However, the conflict is not limited to the opposing kingdoms. Members of each kingdom also argue and fight among themselves.

The Kings and Queens have their own conflicts, while the other pieces struggle with selfishness, mistrust, manipulation, and competing ambitions.

The absence of unity weakens both kingdoms. Rather than working together, their members contribute to their own destruction.

==================================================
4. THE CHARACTERS
==================================================

The character descriptions generally apply to both kingdoms unless otherwise specified. Corresponding characters differ primarily in colour, with some differences in personality.

THE PAWN (THEY/THEM):

- Trust issues.
- Selfish.
- Quiet.
- Weak.

The Pawn is determined to reach the end of the board and become a Queen through promotion. They become so preoccupied with achieving this goal that they neglect loyalty and meaningful connections with others.

By the end of the story, everyone else is dead. The Pawn is the only character left alive, but their ambition has become meaningless in the deserted world.

Ultimately, the Pawn dies by suicide.

THE ROOK (HE/HIM):

- Selfless.
- Depersonalised.
- Loyal.

The Rook's loyalty makes him vulnerable to manipulation by the King, who repeatedly uses him through commands and castling.

The Rook is exploited by the King and eventually sacrifices himself. Before his death, he realises that the King has been using him. However, he realises this too late, as Queen Regina Noir kills him.

THE KNIGHT (HE/HIM):

- Uncaring.
- Has a high ego.
- Cruel.
- Unkind.
- Non-loyal.

The Knight considers himself an exceptional warrior and focuses on defeating his enemies. However, he neglects the horse that remains loyal to him.

The horse eventually dies after being involved in battle for too long. The Knight has taken his horse's loyalty for granted and has become so dependent on him that he cannot function effectively without him, despite carrying a sword himself.

The Knight fails to learn from his behaviour or improve himself. Queen Regina Alba eventually kills him.

THE BISHOP (THEY/THEM):

- High ego.
- Trust issues.
- Hateful.

The Bishop struggles to trust the world around them. Their distrust is reflected symbolically in their diagonal movement, as they repeatedly choose a different route rather than a straight path.

Eventually, a Pawn kills the Bishop with a dagger.

The Bishop also distrusts Queen Regina Alba, despite her being a member of their own kingdom. The Queen's repeated killing contributes to this distrust, and the Bishop eventually kills her.

THE KING (HE/HIM):

- Manipulative.
- Greedy.
- Selfish.
- Bloodthirsty.

The King wants power and control, but his behaviour contributes to conflict within his own kingdom. He repeatedly argues with other members of his side, and the lack of unity eventually contributes to the pieces turning against one another.

The King also bullies the Queen during their arguments. He targets her insecurities and attacks her character, criticising her behaviour and using personal matters against her.

THE QUEEN (SHE/HER):

- Alcoholic.
- Asocial.
- Independent.
- Bloodthirsty.

The Queen's bloodlust is connected to a psychological condition rather than simply being a personality trait.

She feels little or nothing and experiences pleasure from killing people. She repeatedly uses her power to kill, driven by the gratification she experiences from doing so.

Her killing continues until a Bishop from her own kingdom eventually kills her because they do not trust her.

The Queen also frequently argues with the King.

When she is intoxicated or in an angry state associated with her drinking, she throws wine glasses at him without necessarily caring whether they hit him.

During arguments about palace struggles or other matters, however, she sometimes deliberately throws glasses with the intention of hitting him.

The King responds to their arguments by bullying her and targeting her insecurities, including criticising her character.

Their relationship is characterised by hostility, arguments, and aggression.

==================================================
5. THE PLOT IN CHRONOLOGICAL CHAPTER ORDER
==================================================

INTRODUCTION:

The introduction establishes the premise of Chess, introducing Cuboboard, the two kingdoms, and the anthropomorphic chess pieces who inhabit the world.

It establishes the bleak atmosphere and the central conflict surrounding territorial ambition, hatred, and the destructive behaviour of the pieces.

CHAPTER 1: THE ALBI

Introduces the white kingdom, its structure, its members, and its army.

CHAPTER 2: THE NOIRS

Introduces the black kingdom and its members. The Noir kingdom mirrors the Albi in its general structure, while its characters have their own personalities and differences.

CHAPTER 3: QUEEN REGINA ALBA

Focuses on Queen Regina Alba, including her independence, alcoholism, bloodlust, and tendency to kill repeatedly because she experiences pleasure from killing.

Her relationship with the King and her behaviour within the kingdom are also relevant to her character.

CHAPTER 4: ARGUMENTS

Arguments occur throughout the kingdoms.

The King and Queen argue with each other, and the other members of the kingdoms also experience disagreements and conflicts.

The King bullies the Queen during their arguments by targeting her insecurities and criticising her character. The Queen sometimes responds by throwing wine glasses at him, either recklessly or with the deliberate intention of hitting him.

These personal conflicts reflect the wider lack of unity within the kingdoms.

CHAPTER 5: ESCALATION

The arguments and conflicts intensify. Hostility between the characters continues to grow, contributing to deteriorating relationships and increasing instability within the kingdoms.

CHAPTER 6: FIRE

The Albi attack the Noir by firing upon them. This marks a significant escalation in the conflict between the two kingdoms.

CHAPTER 7: NOIR CABALLARIUS

Focuses on the Black Knight, Noir Caballarius.

His horse, which remains loyal to him despite his neglect, dies after being involved in battle for too long.

The Knight has repeatedly taken his horse's loyalty for granted and has failed to recognise how dependent he is on him.

Queen Regina Alba eventually kills the Knight.

CHAPTER 8: PEDO GOALS

The Pawn becomes the focus of the story.

They are determined to reach the end of the board and become a Queen. Their ambition occupies their attention as they pursue promotion.

Their selfishness and distrust contribute to their lack of loyalty towards others.

CHAPTER 9: NOIR REX

The Black King, Noir Rex, responds to the Albi's attack by firing back at them. The conflict between the kingdoms continues to escalate.

CHAPTER 10: EQUES GREED

Focuses on the theme of greed, particularly in relation to the Knight and his behaviour.

The Knight's ego, cruelty, and disregard for his loyal horse contribute to his downfall.

CHAPTER 11: ROCHUS MANIPULATION

Focuses on the Rook and the King's manipulation of him.

The Rook's selflessness and loyalty make him vulnerable to exploitation. The King repeatedly commands and uses him, including through castling.

The Rook eventually sacrifices himself and realises that the King has been using him. However, he realises this too late, and Queen Regina Noir kills him.

CHAPTER 12: EPSICOPUS TRUST

Focuses on the Bishop and their distrust.

The Bishop struggles to trust others and repeatedly chooses indirect routes, reflecting their unwillingness to trust the world around them.

A Pawn eventually kills the Bishop with a dagger.

The Bishop's distrust of Queen Regina Alba is also significant. Despite being on the same side, the Bishop does not trust the Queen because of her repeated killing and eventually kills her.

CHAPTER 13: ALBUS AND NOIR REX

The two Kings, Albus Rex and Noir Rex, engage in a major battle. Their conflict represents a further escalation in the struggle between the two kingdoms.

CHAPTER 14: FATAL SIEGE

Both kingdoms suffer devastating losses. Their armies are reduced to only three Pawns each.

The situation becomes increasingly desperate as the surviving pieces face the consequences of the prolonged conflict.

CHAPTER 15: BATTLES

An intense battle takes place. The fighting reaches an exceptionally destructive level as the remaining pieces continue their conflict.

CHAPTER 16: PEDO ACCOMPLISHMENTS

The remaining characters die, leaving the Pawn as the only character alive.

The Pawn has spent the story pursuing promotion and becoming a Queen. However, by the end, everyone else is dead, and the Pawn is left alone.

Their ambition has consumed them, but the world in which they hoped to achieve their goal has been destroyed by the conflict.

The Pawn eventually dies by suicide.

CHAPTER 17: CUBOBOARD, DESERTED

Cuboboard is left deserted.

The two kingdoms, their armies, and their inhabitants are gone. The territory they fought to control remains, but there is no one left to possess it.

The story ends with the destruction of the societies that sought to conquer the infinite board.

Internal disagreements and hostility undermine both kingdoms from within.

AMBITION:
The Pawn's pursuit of promotion continues even as the world around them collapses.
